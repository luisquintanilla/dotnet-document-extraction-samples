BAOTOU
Baotou is the largest city within the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region in China. It's population of more than 1.6 million primarily consists of Han Chinese. Despite its location in Inner Mongolia, there are only 25,000 ethnic Mongols living in the city, in addition to 28,000 Hui Muslims, 18,000 Manchu, 650 Koreans, 550 Daur and 220 Xibe. Many of the street signs in Baotou are in the Mongolian script, but these are mainly for show only, as the population of the city is overwhelmingly Han, and even many of the people classified as Mongols in the city have lost their cultural identity and mother tongue
Baotou is situated on the banks of the
Yellow River. Baotou means 'land with deer' in Mongolian, although deer have long been hunted to extinction in this part ofthe province. Baotou was a small,
non-descript town clustered in the semi-arid desert until the 1950's, when the railway line from Baotou to Beijing and Yinchuan was constructed and good roads were built to enable to central government to mine the region's coal and iron deposits. Tens of thousands of workers flooded into Baotou, causing it to develop into a large industrialized city almost overnight. Today, Baotou is a geographically huge city. Khan, the great Mongol conqueror of the 13th century. It is located outside the town of Dongsheng, five hours southwest of Baotou. Although five hours might seem a long distance to travel for some, in Inner Mongolia the vast distances and thinly populated grasslands mean it is nearby by local standards. Catholic missionaries first ventured into Inner Mongolia in the 17th century, adapting the Gospel to the needs of the people. In many areas the priests reported many converts, but a closer inspection reveals that often the incentive was an economic one. The Catholics allowed "believers" to farm their land and gave tools and livestock to those who would join them. Modern Protestant work did not get underway until 1895, when the Scandinavian Alliance Mission' sent D. W. Stenberg to the region. He was joined by other workers, many of whom lost their lives during the Boxer Rebellion in 1900.
In 1921 Rev. Li Jinbiao founded the Assembly of
God near Baotou. Using the catch phrase "Fear no poverty, neither hardship nor death", Li soon had more than 4,000 converts in the Baotou area.
